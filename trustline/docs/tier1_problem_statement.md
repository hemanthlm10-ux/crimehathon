# CR-26-047 · Tier 1 Problem Statement · Team Wi-Fight Club.exe · Approach: **DEFENSE**

**What we believe happened.** A compromise inside TallyBridge's environment exposed both of the integration keys Harbor & Finch issued to it (`key-tb-07` for LedgerOne, `tb-sync-svc` for HelpDeskr). From 9 Aug to 6 Sep the keys were used outside the nightly sync. First from home-broadband addresses, then from the vendor's own build server (`tb-build-02`, 10.20.4.77) over the private link. The activity: probing, exporting the vendor master twice, searching support tickets for secrets, and reading bank details and payments. A storage key a support agent had pasted into a ticket (`bvk-7f3a`, TKT-48213) was found and used to read `/finance/bank-instructions` in BlueVault. On **3 Sep** the LedgerOne key changed **V-0412 Coastal Packaging's bank account** with no approval reference, five days before the **8 Sep payment run**. The vendor's "matter closed" notice (E16) is wrong on every factual claim.

**Key evidence (UTC).** 9 Aug: recon from 198.18.7.41 (E01 `req-c28d87e49d`…`req-b7f4b937ed`), using retired client "TallyBridge-Sync/3.4.1" (E20-C: retired 10 Jul) · 11 Aug 17:42: `tb-sync-svc` searches "password/api_key/token/**bvk-**", then 10 export→download→delete cycles (E02 EXP-5410…5419). This matches the overage exactly: Aug invoice 14 jobs = 4 weekly + 10 (E06) · 12 Aug: vendor master exported ×2 (9,840 / 9,835 rows), payments searched (E01 `req-c35c2ccaff`, `req-51b30fba51`) · 14–16 Aug: `bvk-7f3a` from 198.18.7.23/.24/.77 reads vendor-bank-details-2026.xlsx, payment-run-template.xlsx, statements (E05). This key normally only reads /customer-docs from 172.16.9.40 · 15 Aug: 198.18.7.77 fails 3 sign-ins to the IT Security Admin; MFA held (E03, E13) · 16 Aug: vendor "re-provisions" tb-build-02 (E20-B) · 17–19 Aug 12:10: tb-build-02 (E15) uses `key-tb-07` with a spoofed current UA: 45 payment searches, bank-details reads, payments export 4,210 rows + delete (`req-fe4bd4fe93`), ticket searches "bank/iban/swift" · 3 Sep 01:44: bank PATCH on V-0412 (E18 `req-5f61b0de8` = E19 `chg-8b9888c39b`, no approval_ref) hidden inside the new contact-sync writes; 6 Sep: scheduled payments checked again.

**What we are unsure about, and what would settle it.** (1) Whether the 8 Sep payment to V-0412 was released. *Settle:* LedgerOne payment-run record and bank confirmation. (2) How the keys left TallyBridge. The SOC 2 carves out the credential vault and source code (E07). Their job advert seeks to "scan build pipelines for stored secrets" (E20-D). *Settle:* vendor forensics on tb-build-02, VaultWorks/Gitly logs. (3) Whether the keys were ever rotated: E04 (2 Sep) shows both keys created 2024-06-12 and still active, and the key was used on 3 Sep. *Settle:* LedgerOne/HelpDeskr key history. (4) BlueVault times are approximate (unsynced clock), though order and day are reliable. (5) V-0233's bank change (20 Aug, f.dsouza) uses approval ref APR-1182, about 60 numbers older than the refs in use that week. *Settle:* Finance approval register.

**Alternatives considered.** *Legitimate vendor activity / new release:* ruled out. A retired client version, public IPs, a build host outside the six-host sync pool, midday hours, deletes of fresh exports, and secret-keyword searches are not sync behaviour. *Staff export or audit:* Farah's 22 Jul export was announced in chat (E13) and came from the office. Sunita's weekly exports are Tuesdays 09:10 from 172.16.8.12. *Home-broadband staff:* 63 other staff legitimately sign in from 198.18.7.x (E03, E20-G), so the address alone proves nothing. *Former employee (r.menon) / contractor travel / svc-reporting loop:* real hygiene issues, but no link to the integration activity.

**The problem we chose.** *Integration keys are trusted by possession alone.* No system checks whether the software holding a key behaves like the software the key was issued to. Each system sees only its own fragment, so five "small things" were never joined. This is the common factor in every wave, and the one that moves money. Governance fixes (contracts, training, vendor review) are needed but slow. Detection and containment protect the next payment run.

**The obvious approach, and why we are not taking it.** Block the vendor's indicator list (E17) or its /24, add volume-threshold SIEM rules, rotate the keys. On the supplied data the IOC list finds 28% of malicious events. It never mentions the build host or the BlueVault addresses, and its "python-requests" user agent appears in no log. Blocking the /24 raises 291 false alarms on staff home broadband. Volume thresholds find 4% of malicious events. Rotation alone was "done" and the key was still abused on 3 Sep.

**Our approach (TrustLine).** Learn a *behaviour contract* for every non-human identity from its own history: origins, client version, hours, operations, data areas. Check every event against it. Stitch violations across systems using the register's ownership, request IDs, private-link host labels, and "secret searched → secret used". Respond with payment holds before the next run and with a least-privilege re-issue spec derived from the same contract.

**What would change our mind.** TallyBridge showing that the 10.20.4.77 jobs were sanctioned and on our behalf, *and* Coastal Packaging confirming the new account through a call-back on its pre-change number. Or evidence that 198.18.7.41/.58 were H&F staff.
